ABOUT THE EXPERIMENT

Discover the structure of words

Every language has its own way of building words. Many words are made up of smaller meaningful units called morphemes.

These can be roots, prefixes, suffixes, or infixes. By identifying how these pieces combine, we can understand words we have never seen before.

In this experiment, you'll investigate examples from unfamiliar languages. The goal is not to memorise vocabulary, but to develop a reasoning strategy that can be applied to entirely new linguistic systems.

THE RESEARCH QUESTION

What are we trying to find out?

We want to understand whether the way learners receive guidance influences how effectively they discover and apply new linguistic rules.

Interactive AI tutoring vs. static hints

We compare an interactive AI tutor with a version that provides written hints. Both versions are designed to cover the same underlying material. By comparing participants' answers, we can investigate whether interactive guidance helps learners develop stronger reasoning skills.

THE AI TUTOR

Learning through reasoning

In the AI version, a language model acts as an interactive tutor. Rather than simply providing solutions, it is designed to help learners work through problems step by step.

It can ask questions, respond to hypotheses, and provide progressively stronger hints when a learner gets stuck.

No prior knowledge required

Linguistics Olympiad-style problems are designed to be solved through logical reasoning rather than prior knowledge of a particular language. Curiosity and careful observation are all you need.
